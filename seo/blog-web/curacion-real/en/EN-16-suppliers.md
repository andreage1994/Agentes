# Earn 10% more by negotiating with suppliers

Saturday night, the place is packed. Then Monday comes, you open your bank account and the money isn't there. Being full doesn't mean being profitable.

Often, part of that money walks out through the service door: delivery notes nobody checks, prices nobody has renegotiated and purchases spread around without a plan. Let's look at how we help recover up to 10% of margin by negotiating better with your suppliers.

## Negotiating with suppliers

Negotiating isn't something you do once, when you open. It's an ongoing process that decides how competitive you are in the long run.

For us, a supplier is an ally, not an adversary. But for that alliance to work in your favour, you have to sit down to negotiate **with your numbers**.

### The 3Cs of a good supplier relationship

This is how we build agreements that last:

- **Communication:** be clear about the volumes you expect to buy, the quality standards you won't negotiate on and a delivery schedule that also suits the supplier's route.
- **Trust:** it's built by paying on time and keeping your agreements.
- **Commitment:** back the supplier when the market gets tight (for example, when a product is scarce) in exchange for supply priority and stable prices.

### Tactics to recover margin

Saving isn't just about lowering the unit price. There are other levers:

- **Consolidate your purchases.** Buy 80% of what you need from 7 or 8 main suppliers. You'll carry more weight with each of them and can get key-account prices.
- **Keep competition alive.** Have at least two options for critical products such as meat, fish or vegetables. When suppliers know there's an alternative, they sharpen their offer. Consolidating doesn't mean depending on one: the second can be a back-up supplier you buy less from.
- **Negotiate payment terms.** Paying at 30 or 60 days helps your cash flow. If you'd rather pay earlier, ask for an early-payment discount: it can save you 2-3% on the invoice.
- **Fix prices by season.** Agree the price of your 10 to 15 most important products for the whole season. That protects your recipe costs from market swings.

A made-up example, to see it with numbers: if you buy €4,000 a month from a supplier and agree a 2-3% early-payment discount, that's between €80 and €120 a month. Between €960 and €1,440 a year, just for paying earlier.

## How we work on it

We're not office consultants: we come from behind the bar. Our job is to bring order to purchasing so the business can breathe.

### Negotiate with data, not pleas

A supplier is an expert trader and knows their business better than you do. To negotiate as equals, you need your numbers. We show you how to use your purchase history to ask for better terms: if you buy 100 cases of beer a month, you already have something to negotiate with, and you should negotiate accordingly. For that, you need good [inventory control](https://www.thebarnbarconsulting.com/en/restaurant-inventory-control/).

We can work on this part online: you send us the restaurant's numbers and information, and we analyse them without needing to travel. Where we do come in person is to see the operation, the service, the workflow and the team.

### The strength of consolidating

Do you buy meat from three different places to save a few cents? You lose time and bargaining power. One main supplier and a back-up are usually enough. Concentrating your purchases on a few suppliers makes you more important to them, saves on delivery costs and lets you agree payment terms that protect your cash flow.

### A menu that sells what you want it to

Buying better doesn't help much if your menu pushes the dishes that leave the least. We design the [menu](https://www.thebarnbarconsulting.com/en/how-to-create-a-menu-that-actually-sells/) so it sells what you're interested in, not just what customers order out of habit. Highlighting the dishes with the best margin and simplifying the ones that take a lot of work for little profit can raise your profitability by 5%. Watch out for [vampire dishes](https://www.thebarnbarconsulting.com/en/vampire-dishes-restaurant-profitability/).

It's part of how we apply our [BAR Method](https://www.thebarnbarconsulting.com/en/the-bar-method/): looking at purchasing together with the menu and the numbers, not separately.
