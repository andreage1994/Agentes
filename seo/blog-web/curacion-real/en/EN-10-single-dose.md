## ⚠️ Corrección legal (la misma que en español)

El texto publicado en inglés dice que la prohibición entra en vigor **el 12
de agosto de 2026**. **No es así:** las monodosis de plástico en sala se
prohíben desde el **1 de enero de 2030** (Reglamento (UE) 2025/40, art. 25 y
anexo V, punto 4). Además, pone en 2030 solo las miniaturas de hotel, sitúa
el límite del 50 % de espacio vacío en 2028 y mantiene la excepción del
plástico compostable, que en español ya quitamos. Todo se corrige igual que
en el 10, con las fechas comprobadas en el texto oficial (BOE,
DOUE-L-2025-80087).

# The end of single-dose packaging in restaurants?

You've probably read it: "no more ketchup sachets or butter portions". Some headlines even said August 2026. Relax: the ban on plastic single-dose packs for customers eating in arrives on **1 January 2030**.

That doesn't mean you should wait until the last minute. Let's look at what the law actually says, what's banned, what the exceptions are and how to get your bar or restaurant ready, without rushing or overspending.

## What exactly does the regulation say about single-dose packaging in restaurants?

The change comes from Regulation (EU) 2025/40 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 19 December 2024 on packaging and packaging waste, known as the PPWR.

Unlike a directive, which each country has to turn into its own law, an EU regulation applies directly across the whole Union. Spain can't change it as it likes. It affects restaurants, bars, cafés and tourist accommodation.

### The timeline, date by date

The regulation doesn't change everything at once. It goes in stages:

- **12 August 2026: the regulation starts to apply.** Among other things, harmful chemicals such as PFAS are restricted in packaging that touches food.
- **February 2027: penalties and the customer's own container.** Each country must have its penalty system in place. Also, for takeaway food and drinks, customers will be able to bring their own reusable container at no extra cost, and the venue will have to say so clearly.
- **February 2028: reusable packaging for takeaway.** Venues selling takeaway food or drinks will also have to offer a reusable packaging option within a return system. Micro-enterprises are exempt.
- **1 January 2030: goodbye to plastic single-dose packs for eating in.** Single-use plastic single-dose packs are banned for anything consumed on the premises, and so are the mini shampoo and shower gel bottles in hotels, which will have to switch to refillable dispensers. The 50% limit on empty space in transport and e-commerce packaging also starts on that date.

## The products banned for eating in, and the exceptions

The ban targets single-use plastic that reaches the customer's table. From 1 January 2030, if the customer is eating inside your venue (dining room, bar, private rooms or a terrace with table service), you won't be able to give them any of these individual formats if they contain plastic:

- **Coffee creamer and milk pots**, very common at breakfast and in hotels.
- **Sauce and dressing sachets:** ketchup, mayonnaise, mustard, brava sauce, vinegar…
- **Butter, jam, honey, margarine or spread portions.**
- **Sugar, sweetener, salt, pepper or spice sachets** with plastic layers inside to keep moisture out.

### The exceptions

The law leaves a few doors open:

- **Takeaway and delivery.** What the customer takes away or gets delivered is excluded. For delivery and takeaway orders, single-dose sauces or oil can still be used. It makes sense: it's how hot and greasy food travels safely.
- **100% paper sachets.** The ban is on plastic. Sugar, salt or pepper sachets made only of paper, with no plastic coating, are still legal for eating in. Some suppliers already offer papers with a natural greaseproof barrier.
- **Hospitals, clinics and care homes with medical assistance.** They're excluded when single-dose packs are needed for safety and hygiene, because care is given individually for medical reasons.

## Olive oil: cruets that can't be refilled

If one product gives restaurateurs in Spain a headache, it's olive oil. Spain already had its own rule, older than the EU one, and very strict.

### What the olive oil law says

Since 1 January 2014, Royal Decree 895/2013 has banned refillable oil cruets in any bar, restaurant or catering business. Today it's also included in Royal Decree 760/2021, the quality standard for olive oils.

There are two reasons. One, to protect customers from fraud: stopping branded bottles from being refilled with lower-quality oil. Two, to keep the oil's flavour and properties, which are quickly lost with light and air if the container doesn't close properly.

So any olive oil you put on the table or the bar must meet two requirements:

- **Full labelling:** brand, origin, batch and best-before date.
- **A non-refillable closure** that breaks the first time it's opened. The most common are caps with an anti-refill valve and bottles with a safety ring that breaks on the first twist.

## How to prepare your bar or restaurant

There's time until 2030. Use it to change calmly and without overspending:

1. **Take stock of your single-dose packs:** what you use, how much and at what point in service.
2. **Use up your stock wisely.** Don't buy large quantities of formats you'll have to withdraw.
3. **Talk to your suppliers now** about alternatives: dispensers, paper sachets, larger formats. It's a good time to [negotiate with them](https://www.thebarnbarconsulting.com/en/negotiate-restaurant-suppliers/).
4. **Work out the cost.** A well-chosen dispenser can cost less per serving than a single-dose pack. A badly chosen one can send waste through the roof. Run the numbers before you switch, and keep an eye on the [hidden costs of a bar](https://www.thebarnbarconsulting.com/en/hidden-costs-bar/).
5. **Look after dispenser hygiene.** A sauce bottle on the table goes through many hands during the day. Refilling it without cleaning it is a risk. Update your food safety plan to include them: the team has to take apart, wash and disinfect each dispenser before refilling it. This applies to sauces: olive oil has its own rule, and refillable containers aren't allowed there (see above).
6. **Control the dose.** With single-dose packs, the portion came measured from the factory. With a bottle, consumption can shoot up. Choose dispensers with a fixed dose per pump, or serve the sauce in small reusable bowls with just the right amount.
7. **Train the team** so everyone does it the same way, from the first shift to closing.
