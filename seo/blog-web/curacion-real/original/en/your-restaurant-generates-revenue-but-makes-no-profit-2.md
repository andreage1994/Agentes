## 1. The Ghost Menu in PDF Format (The Number One Enemy of Your SEO)

Imagine a potential customer walking down the street on a Thursday at 6:00 PM with a ravenous hunger. They pull out their phone, search for your venue online, and click the link to see what you offer. What they find is a button reading “View Menu” that forces them to download a heavy 15-megabyte PDF file.

On mobile devices, a PDF menu is a cardinal sin of usability and design. It forces users to eat up their data, wait out a slow download, and perform thumb gymnastics with continuous pinching and zooming just to read dish names and prices on a tiny screen. pinch-to-zoom.

## 2. Your Website Loads at a Snail’s Pace and Isn’t Built for Mobile

The patience of a digital consumer is clocked in milliseconds. If your restaurant’s website takes more than 3 seconds to load on a smartphone, over half of your visitors will bounce before seeing a single shot of your food. Google measures this speed with a magnifying glass and penalizes slow sites in its search results, burying them in rankings where nobody clicks. The primary reason hospitality sites crawl is the unoptimized weight of their food photography. Uploading high-res images straight from a photographer’s camera without compression is like trying to run a marathon dragging a sack of cement. Images must be compressed and saved in next-gen formats like WebP or AVIF, stripping their size down from 4 megabytes to under 100 kilobytes without losing a single drop of their visual appetite.

## 3. Forcing Guests to Call You or Slide into Your Instagram DMs to Book

The modern guest hates talking on the phone for routine tasks. Forcing someone to call, wait until the manager has a free second mid-service to pick up, check for an open table, and scribble it down by hand in a paper book is a non-stop source of errors and a massive time drain for your team.

Even worse is lacking an online booking engine and splashing messages on your site like: “To book, send us a DM on Instagram” or “Email us 24 hours in advance.”. This is commercial suicide in an era of immediacy. If a user decides to grab a bite at your spot on a Saturday afternoon and can’t lock down their table instantly, they will leave and look for a venue that gives them real-time confirmation.

## 4. Ignoring the Technical Language Google Speaks (The Absence of Schema Markup)

Here is where we solve the mystery of Carlos, our client from Chamberí who was dropping €8,000 a month because of his website.. His site was beautiful and fast, and it even featured a fantastic booking button. But Carlos made the mistake of leaving his hours, menu, and contact details in plain text without “labeling” them internally for search engines.

Google uses a technical language invisible to human eyes called structured data or Schema Markup.. It’s a hidden snippet of code that tells the search engine explicitly: “This is the restaurant’s opening hours,” “This is the price of the ham croquettes,” and “This is the button to book a table.” This hidden layout is exactly what allows Google maps to display your live schedules flawlessly instead of blacklisting your traffic.

## 5. Artificial Stock Photos and a Total Lack of Real Shots of Your Food

Is there anything sadder than entering the website of a pizzeria in Madrid and seeing a stock photo of a New York model biting into a hyper-perfect, industrial slice of pizza? Generic catalog images strip away all the personality and authenticity of your venue. It stands out from miles away that they aren’t real, and guests instantly assume that if your photos are fake, your dishes will be too.

## 6. Hiding Your Contact Info and Forgetting to Tell Guests What to Do (Hidden CTAs)

Sometimes web designers get a bit too modern and forget the absolute basics: making it easy for the user. It’s incredibly common to find restaurant sites with beautiful minimalist designs where hunting down the address, phone number, or opening hours turns into a headache.

The customer doesn’t want to play detective or run an investigation. They want to land on your site and answer three questions in three seconds: “Where is it?”, “When does it open?”, and “How do I book?” .
